This course introduces students to fundamental concepts and theories within sport and exercise psychology. The goal is to provide a broad overview of major topics within the area of physical activity psychology — covering human cognition, emotion, and behaviour within physical activity contexts.

Knowledge of sport and exercise psychology theory and applications will assist students in understanding underlying psychological processes as they relate to physical activity performance and participation. These skills are relevant to many kinesiology and health-related professional practices.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

  • Describe sport and exercise psychology theories and constructs and discuss their relevance to physical activity contexts.
  • Apply theoretical perspectives and constructs to case studies and provide recommendations for intervention and applied practice.
  • Critically assess sport and exercise psychology theory, constructs, and knowledge application.

In-Person Seminars (×2)
In class · 5% each · see syllabus for dates
10%

Each seminar runs for the first hour of class (2:00–3:00 PM), followed by the regular lecture. Students engage in small-group, seminar-style discussions applying course concepts to sport and physical activity contexts — no written preparation is required, but you should come having completed the assigned readings and ready to contribute.

Seminar #1: Topic — The Theory of Planned Behaviour (Ch. 3). Small groups explore what the TPB explains well about physical activity participation and where it falls short, including whose barriers it may struggle to see.

Seminar #2: Topic — Physical Activity Across the Lifespan (Ch. 10 & 12). Groups explore how frameworks for youth and aging compare, and what assumptions about the body and participation are built into each.

Grading (completion-based): Each seminar requires (1) attendance confirmed via sign-in sheet, and (2) a brief Canvas reflection submitted by 11:59 PM on the day of the seminar — responding to one prompt: what did you find particularly engaging or challenging? Both must be completed to receive marks.

If a seminar is missed due to a documented medical issue or personal emergency, the 5% will be transferred to the final exam, consistent with the academic concession policy.
Applied Reflection
Canvas submission (Turnitin-enabled) · 500–700 words · see syllabus for due date
10%

Select one concept or theory from Chapters 2–4 (Personality, Motivation & Behavioural Change, or Stress, Emotion & Coping) and apply it to a real sport, exercise, or movement experience — either your own or one you have observed.

The goal is not to write a perfect academic essay — it is to show that you understand a concept well enough to recognize it in the real world and to think critically about what it does and doesn't explain. Write in your own voice.

Your paper should:

  • Clearly identify and accurately describe the theory or concept in your own words
  • Connect it to a specific, real example from sport, exercise, or movement
  • Reflect on what the theory explains well and where it falls short or feels incomplete
  • Draw on at least one source beyond the textbook (e.g., a peer-reviewed article)

Example directions (illustrative, not exhaustive): A personality trait or construct and how it shows up in sport · A motivational theory applied to your own physical activity relationship · A coping strategy applied to a stressor you've encountered in sport or exercise.

⚠️ AI Use Policy: Generative AI is not permitted at any stage — including brainstorming, outlining, or drafting. All submissions are reviewed through Turnitin. Any use of AI will be treated as academic misconduct per UBC policy.

Case Study Exam
In class · Written, in-person · 75 minutes · see syllabus for date
20%

You will choose one of three provided case studies and write an analytical response demonstrating your ability to apply course theory to a real physical activity context. You will have one week to read, think about, and sit with the cases — your response should reflect genuine analytical engagement, not a summary of the case.

No materials permitted (notes, readings, or textbook). Write clearly and in your own voice — you are assessed on the quality of your thinking, not grammar or academic language.

Each response must:

  • Identify and explain the relevant theoretical framework in your own words
  • Apply that framework specifically to the person and situation described
  • Address the specific prompts at the end of your chosen case

Note: The case studies engage with themes that may feel personally relevant — including experiences of exclusion in physical activity spaces, sport culture and identity, and how systems respond to people who may not fit neatly into their defaults. If you would prefer an alternative case, contact the instructor no later than one week before the exam.

If you miss the case study exam due to a documented medical issue or personal emergency, the 20% weighting will be transferred to the final exam.

Assessment Policies

Late Assignments

Late assignments are deducted at 10% per day (including weekends). Assignments are not accepted after 6 days. Submit all work via Canvas or in person during lecture — not by email, unless otherwise agreed with the instructor.

Academic Concession

Due dates and exams will not be rescheduled except for a medical issue, personal emergency, or academic conflict. If you miss a midterm or case study exam, the weighting transfers to the final exam. Contact the instructor as soon as possible.

Marking Discussions

Contact the TA no sooner than 24 hours and no later than one week after the assignment is returned. If concerns remain, submit a re-grade request to the instructor within one week of speaking with the TA. Re-graded work may receive a higher, lower, or the same mark.
